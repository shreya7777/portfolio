# WeCollab

## My Role

WeCollab is an EdTech concept I designed as part of an Indiana University industry project (Oct–Dec 2024) — connecting students, instructors, and clients through a streamlined Project-Based Learning ecosystem. Students find real-world projects, clients submit and track proposals, and instructors manage the collaboration end to end. My role: UX Researcher & Designer, running the work from early research through to a pitch-ready, validated concept.

- 11 | Research participants | 9 students, 1 instructor, and 1 client.
- 8.6 | NPS score | From usability testing across all three user groups.
- $450K | Projected Year 3 revenue | Based on a subscription and licensing business model.

## Problem & Research

> Stakeholders in PBL/SL settings are looking to align educational and business goals but they lack the ability to create a symbiotic experience because of unstructured workflows. Therefore, meaningful contributions are not made.

Analyzing the environment around students, instructors, and clients surfaced where their needs overlapped — and where market pressure was building.

![Where students, clients, and instructors' needs overlap](Work/wecollab/Issues.png)

- 55% | Multidisciplinary approach | Programs involve multiple disciplines, with higher adoption in engineering.
- 46% | Project fees | Programs charge a fee, averaging $4,449 in 2024.
- 52% | Formal support structures | Institutions have dedicated experiential learning offices, particularly in business and engineering departments.
- 53% | Real-world client involvement | Programs incorporate live clients, increasing every year.
- 61% | Outcome measurement | Institutes track learning impact to improve program effectiveness.

From there, the process ran secondary research through to a pitch-ready concept:

- Research | Secondary research, environmental analysis, and user interviews.
- Data Analysis | Value proposition canvas, business model canvas, and TAM analysis.
- Brainstorming | Generated 3 ideas and storyboarded the strongest concept.
- Testing | Ran think-aloud usability testing on the prototype.
- Pitch | Presented the validated concept and business case.

Ran user interviews, task analysis, and a post-test evaluation with 9 students, 1 instructor, and 1 client — gathering expectations, pain points, and priorities, then watching them navigate the Client Project Portal and dashboard tools before prioritizing improvements for the final prototype.

## Platform & Validation

Here's how the platform works end to end — from what each role puts in, to what they get back out.

![WeCollab platform architecture, from input to output](Work/wecollab/Design Validation Presentation.pdf.png)

- Input | Students submit proposals; instructors approve matches and grade; clients provide project briefs.
- Matching & Workflow | A centralized matching engine plus workflow tools — drive, calendar, chat, progress tracker, and more.
- Backend & Analytics | Cloud/edge computing for scale, data intelligence on match and task metrics, and visualization dashboards.
- Role-Based Output | Students get proposal & feedback dashboards; instructors get oversight tools; clients get deliverable tracking.

Testing Iteration 1 surfaced what was working — and what wasn't — across all three roles.

![Iteration 1 validation across instructor, student, and client views](Work/wecollab/iteration-1-validation.png)

- Instructor App | Merit-based matches and workflow tracking worked well, but evaluations were manual with no skill tracking — fixed by automating evaluation and outcome tracking.
- Student App | Aggregated docs and grading integration worked, but there were no team spaces or real-time feedback — fixed by adding profiles, team spaces, and rejection feedback.
- Client View | Proposals were easy and past projects showcased well, but there was no messaging or status updates — fixed by adding in-platform messaging and proposal tracking.

## Results

Validating the concept meant checking it against desirability, feasibility, and viability.

- Desirability | Students valued the fairness of merit-based admission; clients valued detailed proposals; user-friendly workflows mattered most to both.
- Technical Feasibility | Clients found the portal intuitive but wanted calendar integration and deeper communication; students noted a learning curve for workflows.
- Business Viability | Clients saw value in matching but wanted tiered commitment options; instructors valued dual-layer approval for quality control.

> "The platform made collaboration easy to manage. Would use this as a client." — Client

> "The platform has potential — it's just a matter of ironing out the minor details." — Instructor

The business model was built around a 3rd-party subscription (Basic $500/project, Premium $1,500/project), institution licensing ($10K/institution/year), and add-ons like certifications and advanced tools. Costs ran about $50K to build plus roughly $120K/year to maintain and market.

- $55K | Year 1 profit | $225K revenue against $170K in costs.
- $180K | Year 2 profit | $350K revenue, costs held flat.
- $280K | Year 3 profit | $450K revenue — steady growth year over year.
